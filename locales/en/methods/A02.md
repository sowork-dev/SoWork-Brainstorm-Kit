## A02 | 6-3-5 Brainwriting

**Core setup**: Six participants, each writing three entries per round, with five minutes per round. They then pass their sheets to the next person to build on one another’s ideas. Miro’s explanation of the method supports this setup.[3]

**Opening**: “Write independently first, rather than trying to persuade one another out loud. When you receive someone else’s sheet, you can build on an idea, combine ideas, or suggest an alternative.”

**Process**:
1. Agree on a single prompt. Prepare six forms, each with three columns and space to record six rounds. Record the contributors and round numbers.
2. In the first five-minute round, each person independently writes three entries. Make them clear enough for the next person to understand.
3. Everyone passes their sheet in the same direction. In the next round, read it first, then add three entries in a new row. Keep earlier entries; do not overwrite them.
4. This card uses an initial round plus five passes, for six rounds in total, each lasting five minutes. Leave unfilled slots blank rather than pretending they were completed.
5. Collect the sheets and merge genuine duplicates while preserving meaningful differences. Participants choose directions worth developing.

**Follow-up**: “What part of the previous entry does your addition change: the audience, the format, or an obstacle it addresses?”

**Output**: Idea sheets that show how ideas developed through the relay, plus candidate directions. Fully completed sheets contain 108 filled slots, not necessarily 108 unique or useful ideas.

**AI boundaries**: In the team version, human participants supply each round’s content; AI only organizes and facilitates. For one person, call it an “individual relay-rewriting adaptation.” AI simulations of other roles do not count as formal participation by six people.
