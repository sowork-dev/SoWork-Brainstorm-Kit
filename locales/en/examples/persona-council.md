# Low-effort persona council | Fictional example

**This is an authored product demonstration, not a ChatGPT, API, or real-workshop test transcript. All character dialogue is fictional.**

**User:** Our neighborhood bookshop's weekend events only draw regulars. I want first-time visitors. Pick the people and brainstorm for me.

**Facilitator:** I'll use Across the Ages: Qin Shi Huang handles simple rules, Yang Guifei creates a reason to linger, and David Bowie reframes who the visitor gets to be. I'll assume a small event using the existing shop. These are fictional AI perspectives, not authentic speech or endorsements.

### Qin Shi Huang — A question relay

Give each newcomer one task: draw a question left by someone else, find a book that might answer it, and leave a new question. Keep the entrance, task, and completion rule consistent so staff do not have to explain a different activity to each person.

### Yang Guifei — A one-line memory corner

Create a comfortable spot where visitors pick a sentence from a book and add a small memory card. Nobody has to speak publicly; they can leave a fragment for the next person.

### David Bowie — Tonight, you are not a reader

Offer a fictional role such as a visitor from the future or a detective with no memory. Each person gives a book a new title from that perspective. You do not need to know the bookshop's culture to take part.

### Cross-improvements

- **Yang responds to Qin:** Finding the right book could feel like a test. Start with a small browsing selection and let the question be something open, such as a place you wish you could escape to.
- **Qin responds to Bowie:** Too many roles will create an explanation problem. Start with three; each gets one clear action on an entrance card.
- **Bowie combines them:** Turn the memory card into a message from another identity. Let the visitor take away a stranger's response, not merely watch an event.

### Synthesis

1. **Question relay:** clear rules and an ongoing trail of interaction.
2. **Memory exchange:** the easiest option to test in an existing corner.
3. **Identity retitling:** the most distinctive route, with a little more facilitation.

**Facilitator:** I recommend 2 first. Use a few blank cards and books already available. Test whether first-time visitors willingly leave and take a card; do not assume a conversion rate. You can just say Continue, Swap cast, Go bolder, Make it practical, or You decide.

**User:** Make it practical.

**Facilitator:** Same goal and cast—no new interview. Qin replaces specially printed entrance cards with plain notes: write a line, exchange a line. Yang keeps one welcoming corner. Bowie turns the fictional identity into a short prompt on plain paper rather than costumes or props. Try that minimal version and gather real reactions.
